EVIDENCE 3 - LITTLE'S LAW (the decisive quantitative test): tps == concurrency / latency, EXACTLY:
      c    tps      lat_ms   c/lat_s
      1    1085     0.922    1085
      8    2992     2.674    2992
      16   2385     6.709    2385
      32   2509     12.755   2509
      48   2544     18.867   2544
      64   2445     26.174   2445
  Throughput is flat because per-request round-trip LATENCY rises LINEARLY with concurrency. A
  saturating resource (disk IOPS / a lock / single txg-sync) would hold latency ~flat and cap tps
  at the resource's service rate; instead latency grows linearly = a FIXED round-trip serialized
  through a bottleneck of constant service time, time-shared across the ~4 clumped CPUs. That fixed
  cost is the wake round-trip, NOT a write resource.

VERDICT: (a) WAKE-BOUND. The RW plateau is the SAME wake-placement ceiling as RO, NOT a
         write-specific serialization.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Backends block in sched::thread::switch_to (idle-wait), clumped on ~4 CPUs, 43 vCPUs idle,
    host 94% idle - identical signature to the RO plateau profile (RESULT-matched-AB.txt).
  - The ZFS write path (ZIL commit, txg-sync/dp_sync_taskq, z_wr_iss/z_wr_int zio pipeline) is
    NOT the bottleneck: those threads are idle-blocked with negligible cpu_time; NO backend waits
    on them; no zil/txg/spa/zio_wait/lock appears in the blocked set. fsync-to-NVMe is not the cap
    (Little's Law shows a fixed round-trip, not a saturated device).
  - ROOT CAUSE (shared with RO): core/sched.cc thread::wake_impl re-queues a woken thread onto its
    home/last CPU (st->_cpu -> tcpu->incoming_wakeups[c]); there is NO wake-time idle-CPU steering,
    and the only rebalancer is the lazy 100ms/1-thread load_balance. A single virtio-net RX receiver
    thread (pinned cpu1) wakes each backend; woken backends land on/near the waker's CPU -> the ~4-CPU
    clump; aggregate throughput = what a handful of CPUs push through the wake round-trip, independent
    of vCPU count -> a flat-but-low plateau. RW just adds per-txn fsync LATENCY on top (higher c1
    latency), which is why the RW plateau (~2500) sits below where a pure-CPU workload would, but the
    SHAPE and the CEILING mechanism are the wake-placement one.
  - Contributing (secondary, not the cause): OSv BSD taskqueue creates ZFS zio/taskq worker threads
    UNPINNED (bsd/sys/kern/subr_taskqueue.c taskqueue_start_threads: the sched_prio/sched_add/pin
    block is #if 0), and they too clump (all zvol_tq/dp_sync on cpu1). This is downstream of the same
    missing wake-placement; it does not itself serialize the plateau (those threads are idle here).
