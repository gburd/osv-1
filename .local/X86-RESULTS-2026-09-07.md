## The ZFS COW write ceiling: my standing prior was ~9x TOO OPTIMISTIC

| checkpoint | buffers | data | time | throughput |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| post-recovery | 1,156,986 (55.2%) | 9039 MiB | 123.554 s | 73.2 MiB/s |
| mid-load | 614,505 (29.3%) | 4801 MiB | 89.687 s | 53.5 MiB/s |
| pre-crash | 847,791 (40.4%) | 6623 MiB | 128.436 s | 51.6 MiB/s |
| **aggregate** | | **20463 MiB** | **341.7 s** | **59.9 MiB/s** |

Host device capability on the same NVMe: **883 MB/s** at 1 MiB `oflag=direct`, but only
**107 MB/s** at 8 KiB `oflag=direct,dsync` (WAL-like).

So the guest achieved ~60 MiB/s against a 107 MB/s achievable ceiling = **~56% of
achievable**, with `sync=0.020 s` throughout - **fsync is cheap; the WRITE phase dominates**
(123-128 s). The bottleneck is ZFS COW write amplification at `recordsize=8k` against a
device whose 8K-sync ceiling is already 8x below its streaming rate. vCPU was never the
limit.

**Correction to the standing prior:** the docs said "ZFS COW caps ~500-530 MB/s". Measured
here: ~60 MiB/s. The old figure was ~9x optimistic. Note the aarch64 arm found the SAME
`LWLock:WALWrite` ceiling with a 98%-IDLE device, so the two arms disagree about where the
limit sits and both measurements should be kept: x86 hit a device/COW amplification wall,
aarch64 hit a guest software serialization wall. Do not merge them into one story.

Not measurable: guest CPU idle%, ZFS ARC/txg internals, PG wait events at VU32/VU96 - all
require a run surviving to steady state.

## Two upstream-relevant substrate bugs found
- **`-M q35`** is what hangs OSv, not the qemu version (4-way matrix, machine-type-following,
  accelerator-independent). AL2023 qemu 11 is unbootable *because* it ships only q35.
- **In-guest `initdb` is impossible: OSv stubs `popen()`.** `initdb` shells out to
  `"postgres" -V` and gets `popen() stubbed` -> `program "postgres" is needed by initdb but
  was not found`. So my preferred "initdb in the guest" instruction was unworkable; the
  agent initdb'd on the host, baked it read-only at `/data-seed`, and `seed_copy`'d it into
  the ZFS dataset. No cpiod, no ramfs.
