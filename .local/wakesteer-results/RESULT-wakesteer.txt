=== CRITICAL FINDING (honest, negative for the standalone fix as-is) ===
1. THE REAL LEVER WAS #1464 (sched-load-balance), NOT wake-steering. On the pre-#1464
   substrate the matched run measured a ~12k RO plateau with backends clumped on ~5 of 48
   CPUs. With #1464 alone the plateau is ~32k and 15 CPUs are busy: the 10ms/drain-the-whole-
   imbalance load_balance already spreads the woken-backend clump. Wake-time steering on top
   of #1464 adds ~0 to the RO curve (B/A ~= 1.00 every cell).
2. WAKE-STEERING REGRESSES THE WRITE PATH. With steering ON, pgbench -i at moderate+ scale
   (>= s50, and re-init at s20) LIVELOCKS: the guest spins ~242% CPU and the heavy concurrent
   COPY/index write never completes (steer OFF completes s300 init in ~43s). The read-only
   path is stable; the write path is not. => not safe to land as-is.
3. Implementing it safely required, in order: export_runtime from the HOME cpu scale (not the
   waker's) [a prior "no-export" attempt wedged]; skip status::sending_lock wakeups (lock
   hand-off); confine steering to wake() thread context, never the IRQ-path
   wake_with_irq_disabled() [nested-reschedule exception_depth assert]; and app-threads-only
   [kernel workers page-pool/reaper/taskqueue tripped ran_for's _renormalize_count assert and
   crashed the seed boot]. Even after all four guards, RO is a null and RW livelocks.

=== ROADMAP IMPLICATION ===
The plateau (OSv RO ~32k vs Linux ~190k, with #1464) is NOT wake-placement once #1464 is in.
The residual ceiling is elsewhere: the SINGLE virtio-net RX receiver thread funneling all
inbound (now the next lever, MQ becomes relevant since #1464 already provides idle CPUs), and
loopback/SLIRP + fork-COW overheads. Wake-steering is the WRONG next lever on the #1464 base.
RECOMMENDATION: do NOT open the wake-steer PR (redundant vs #1464 for RO, RW-livelock bug).
Ship #1464 as the sched improvement (already on pr/sched-load-balance). Re-evaluate MQ RX +
per-packet-alloc on the #1464 base as the next throughput lever.
