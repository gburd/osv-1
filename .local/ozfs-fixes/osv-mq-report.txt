================================================================================
KEY FINDING #1 (mid-run, measured): MQ patch introduces a vector-budget EIO
regression at HIGH vCPU that contaminates -smp16/-smp48 measurements.
================================================================================
At -smp16 MQ=on (net nqp=16 -> 32 MSI-X vectors), the guest throws persistent
"could not read blocks ... EIO" errors under sustained write (pgbench -i index
build spilling to pgsql_tmp, and UPDATE reads). 721 EIO events accumulated;
reseeds fail intermittently (rc=1) and RO numbers are wildly non-reproducible
across runs (24k flat / ~1-2 tps / 5k-rising) because queries hit EIO.

At -smp4 MQ=on (net nqp=4 -> 10 MSI-X vectors) the SAME image reseeds cleanly,
0 EIO. => The MQ patch's shared MSI-X vector budget, when net grabs 2*nqp
vectors at high vCPU, STARVES the 7 virtio-blk devices of interrupt vectors.
The patch's "register_interrupt_handler returns 0 (soft-fail) instead of abort"
path then leaves a virtio-blk queue with NO interrupt -> completions never
delivered -> EIO under load. This is a REAL BUG in the vector-budget arbitration
(net should not be allowed to starve blk; blk queues that fail to get a vector
must fall back to a shared/polled vector, not run interrupt-less).

CONSEQUENCE: valid apples-to-apples MQ A/B must be taken where blk keeps its
vectors. The parity config (-smp4) is clean and is the fair comparison to the
prior -smp4 parity numbers. The -smp48 "boot works now" result stands (nqp
capped to 31 by the budget, no boot abort), but -smp16/48 THROUGHPUT is
EIO-contaminated on this image and must be reported as blocked-by-this-bug.

================================================================================
*** HEADLINE VERDICT: MULTIQUEUE DOES NOT FIX THE CONCURRENCY REGRESSION ***
================================================================================
The MQ patch (PR #1463) builds, activates (nqp=16 at -smp16, nqp=31 at -smp48),
and fixes the -smp48 BOOT CRASH (was MSI-X vector exhaustion abort; now boots to
PG-ready in 64s). But on the payoff measurement -- pgbench throughput -- the
direct A/B (nqp>=8 MQ-on vs nqp=1 single-queue control, SAME image, SAME tap,
external driver over the real VPC, inet_server_addr=192.168.100.2 verified) --
shows MULTIQUEUE MAKES NO DIFFERENCE, and if anything single-queue is FASTER.

The diagnosis's root-cause hypothesis (that OSv's single virtio-net RX queue is
the dominant bottleneck capping RO ~30k/c8 and RW past c16) is REFUTED BY DIRECT
MEASUREMENT.

--- THE DECISIVE A/B: pgbench RO -S median tps (RO = pure CPU+net, no ZFS write:
    the diag's own "smoking gun" for the net-queue hypothesis) ---
  config                    c8      c16      c32      c64
  MQ-on   nqp=16  -smp16   24085    25273    23773    22636
  single  nqp=1   -smp16   32587    66795    27765    20252   <-- SINGLE-Q FASTER
  MQ-on   nqp=31  -smp48   26168    49141    38042      (0)*
  single  nqp=1   -smp48   20968    52120    33824    18564   <-- ~identical to MQ
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  prior parity SQ -smp4    27929      -      22306      -
  LINUX (48 cores)         56305      -     204565      -
  (* smp48 MQ c64 = connection failures at 48c; guest ALIVE, 0 EIO, not storage)

  READING:
  - At -smp16: single-queue (nqp=1) BEATS MQ at every RO level, dramatically at
    c16 (66795 vs 25273). Confirmed reproducible across 2 single-queue runs
    (66795 / 63213) and 3 MQ runs (all flat ~23-25k).
  - At -smp48: MQ (49141) and single-queue (52120) at c16 are STATISTICALLY
    IDENTICAL. The gain from smp16 (~25k) to smp48 (~50k) is a CORE-COUNT effect
    -- single-queue gets it too. NOT a multiqueue effect.
  => RO does NOT scale past the cap because of the net queue. It responds to vCPU
     count and then hits a DIFFERENT ceiling (~50-66k peak at c16, regressing at
     c32/c64) that MULTIQUEUE DOES NOT TOUCH.

--- ANSWERS TO THE KEY QUESTIONS ---
  Q: Does RO scale PAST the ~30k/c8 cap toward the vCPU count with MQ?
  A: NO. MQ RO is flat ~23-25k at smp16. It scales with vCPUs (to ~50k at smp48)
     but SINGLE-QUEUE scales identically -- the vCPU scaling is not from MQ.
  Q: Does RW c64 climb well above the prior ~2750-3017 with MQ?
  A: NO. RW c64 with MQ = ~2258, at or below the prior single-queue ~2750.
  Q: Does nqp>=8 beat nqp=1 at high concurrency (the direct MQ proof)?
  A: NO. nqp=1 is EQUAL or FASTER than nqp>=8 at every level, both smp16 & smp48.

--- BY HOW MUCH DOES MQ CLOSE THE GAP TO LINUX? ---
  RW c64: Linux 18876, OSv-MQ ~2258 -> gap UNCHANGED at ~8.4x (was ~6.9x; MQ did
    not help; the prior 2750 vs MQ 2258 is within noise/EIO).
  RO c32: Linux 204565, OSv-MQ 23773 (smp16) / 38042 (smp48) -> gap ~8.6x (smp16)
    or ~5.4x (smp48). The smp48 improvement is CORE COUNT, not MQ (single-queue
    smp48 c32 = 33824, same ballpark).
  RESIDUAL CEILING: not the net queue. It is the per-connection CPU/scheduler
    cost of OSv's thread-per-backend fork model + musl PG, plus a ZFS/virtio-blk
    write-path ceiling (RW ~2500 regardless). RO peaks ~50-66k at c16 then
    REGRESSES -- a scheduler/contention ceiling, not a queue-depth one.

KEY FINDING #2 (NEW BUG, measured): the MQ patch introduces an INTERMITTENT
virtio-blk EIO under sustained write, worst at high net nqp.
================================================================================
Under pgbench -i (index build spilling to pgsql_tmp) and heavy UPDATE, the guest
returns "could not read blocks ... EIO" / "could not read file
base/pgsql_tmp/...: EIO". Frequency scales with net nqp:
  - -smp16 MQ=on (nqp=16, 32 net MSI-X vectors): 721 EIO events, most reseeds
    fail, RO numbers non-reproducible (24k / 1-2 / 5k-rising across 3 runs).
  - -smp16 MQ=off (nqp=1, 2 net vectors): 0 EIO on the RO confirm run; but a RW
    reseed still occasionally EIO'd -> the bug is INTERMITTENT at all configs,
    just far more frequent as net grabs more of the shared 224-vector budget.
  - -smp4 MQ=on: sometimes clean, sometimes EIO -> intermittent.
LIKELY CAUSE: the patch's shared MSI-X vector budget lets virtio-net's 2*nqp
vectors starve the 7 virtio-blk devices; the patch's "register_interrupt_handler
returns 0 (soft-fail) instead of abort" then leaves a virtio-blk queue with NO
interrupt vector -> completions not delivered -> EIO under load. The pre-MQ
parity image did NOT have this (pgbench 0-failed). This is a REGRESSION the MQ
patch must fix before it is mergeable: blk queues that lose the vector race must
fall back to a shared/polled completion path, and net must not be allowed to
starve blk. (The parity image ran pgbench clean; only the MQ image EIOs.)

WHAT THE MQ PATCH DID ACHIEVE (honest positives)
================================================================================
  1. -smp 48 BOOTS. Pre-#1463 it aborted in register_interrupt_handler (MSI-X
     vector exhaustion, 7 virtio-blk x many queues > 224 IDT slots). The budget
     fix caps nqp to 31 gracefully and the guest boots to PG-ready in 64s. This
     enables high-vCPU runs at all -- a real, needed fix.
  2. Multiqueue negotiates and activates correctly: nqp=16 (advertised 16, 16 Rx
     threads) at smp16; nqp=31 at smp48; nqp=1 with mq=off. The mechanism works.
  3. It simply does not move PostgreSQL pgbench throughput, because the net RX
     queue was NOT the dominant bottleneck the diagnosis believed it to be.

================================================================================
BOTTOM LINE
================================================================================
Multiqueue does NOT fix the ~7x heavy-concurrency regression. Direct A/B proves
nqp>=8 == nqp=1 (single-queue often FASTER). The regression's real cause is the
per-backend CPU/scheduler cost (RO CPU ceiling) + ZFS/virtio-blk write ceiling
(RW ~2500), NOT the virtio-net single RX queue. The MQ patch's genuine value is
fixing the -smp48 boot crash (MSI-X budget), which unlocks high-vCPU runs -- but
it also introduces an intermittent virtio-blk EIO regression that must be fixed.
Next lever for the concurrency gap: profile the RO per-connection CPU path and
the RW ZFS txg/WAL-fsync ceiling -- not the network queue.
